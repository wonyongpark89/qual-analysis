Stage 2 of 4: You've coded the transcript. Now move from individual codes to broader categories.
Definition — Category

A category is composed of codes that "look alike and feel alike." Categories are formed by grouping codes into patterns. Like a code, a category is a word or short phrase — but more condensed and operating at a higher level of abstraction.

Look across all your codes and drag those that look and feel alike into a category. Name each one and describe what holds them together. Not every code needs to go into a category — leftover codes are fine.

Stage 3 of 4: You've built categories. Now test them — do the extracts really fit where you've placed them?
Definition — Category

A category is composed of codes that "look alike and feel alike." Categories are formed by grouping codes into patterns. Like a code, a category is a word or short phrase — but more condensed and operating at a higher level of abstraction.

Test each category against the actual data. Do all the extracts really fit? Is anything out of place? Rename categories and move codes between them if needed, then submit.

Stage 4 of 4: You've refined your categories. Now step back and identify the overarching theme — what does this data tell us?
Definition — Theme

A theme is an extended phrase or sentence that identifies what a unit of data is about and/or what it means. Compared with categories, themes are more interpretive, more descriptive, and often more subtle.

Use your finalised categories as the raw material for a theme. A theme isn't a summary — it's an interpretation. What is the data telling you? Write a sentence or phrase that captures the overarching meaning.

Your finalised categories

Read each category and its extracts carefully before writing your theme.

Write your theme

Questions to guide your writing
  • What is the central idea that connects these categories?
  • What is going on here conceptually — not just descriptively?
  • How do these categories relate to each other?
  • Do they represent stages, tensions, causes, or consequences?
  • What question does this theme answer?
